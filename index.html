🚂 The Big Secret: The Two Calendar Trains

START HERE

Imagine two trains running at the exact same speed (365 days a year). Why don't their dates match? Because they celebrate New Year's Day in different seasons:

• Miladi (Gregorian) starts on January 1 (in the middle of Winter, which is 11 Dey in Shamsi).
• Shamsi (Jalali) waits until the first day of Spring (1 Farvardin = March 20 or 21) to start its new year.
• Because Shamsi starts around the 21st of a Miladi month, every single Shamsi month bridges TWO Miladi months (starting around the 21st of one month and ending around the 20th of the next).

❄️ The Winter Overlap (80 Days)
Shamsi 10/11 → 12/29 (Jan 1 → Mar 20)

1. The Year Rule (+621 or +622)

STEP 1: YEAR

Think of it like two friends' birthdays. From March 21 to December 31, both calendars have already had their birthday this year, so the age gap is 621. But on January 1 (11 Dey), Miladi turns a year older while Shamsi hasn't had its birthday yet—so for those 80 winter days, the gap becomes 622!

Shamsi → Miladi: Months 1 to 9 (or Month 10, Days 1–10) Shamsi Year + 621
Shamsi → Miladi: Month 10 (Days 11–30), or Months 11 & 12 Shamsi Year + 622
Miladi → Shamsi: Months 1 & 2 (or Month 3, Days 1–20) Miladi Year − 622
Miladi → Shamsi: Month 3 (Days 21–31), or Months 4 to 12 Miladi Year − 621
🧠 The "3 Baby Jumps" Mental Trick (Never Do Hard Math!):
Don't add or subtract 621 all at once! Break it into 600, then 20, then 1:
• 1405 + 621 → 1405 + 600 = 2005 → + 20 = 2025 → + 1 = 2026!
• 2026 − 621 → 2026 − 600 = 1426 → − 20 = 1406 → − 1 = 1405!

2. The "12-Hour Clock" Starter Month (±3)

STEP 2: MONTH

You never need to memorize month names—just use month numbers 1 to 12. Because Spring (Month 1 of Shamsi) starts 3 months after January (Month 1 of Miladi), the two calendars are 3 months apart.

🕰️ The 12-Hour Clock Rule:
A calendar only goes up to 12, just like a wall clock! If adding 3 goes past 12 (like 11 + 3 = 14), subtract 12 to wrap around the clock (14 − 12 = 2).
Important: Call this result your Starter Month. Hold it in your hand while you check Step 3, because the day might ask to borrow 1 month from it!
Shamsi → Miladi (Months 1–9) Starter Month = Month + 3
Shamsi → Miladi (Months 10–12, wraps past 12) Starter Month = Month + 3 − 12 (or Month − 9)
Miladi → Shamsi (Months 4–12) Starter Month = Month − 3
Miladi → Shamsi (Months 1–3, wraps behind 1) Starter Month = Month + 9 (i.e. Month − 3 + 12)

3. The Day Rule, "Piggy Bank Borrow" & "Overflow Cup"

STEP 3: DAY

First, do a simple subtraction or addition on the Day. Why does the number change from 9 to 10 halfway through the year? Because the first 6 Shamsi months have 31 days, pushing the second half of the year back by 1 extra day!

Shamsi → Miladi: First Half (Shamsi Months 1 to 6) Day − 9
Shamsi → Miladi: Second Half (Shamsi Months 7 to 12) Day − 10
Miladi → Shamsi: Winter (Miladi Months 1 to 3) Day + 9
Miladi → Shamsi: Spring–Winter (Miladi Months 4 to 12) Day + 10

Now, what if your Day becomes 0, Negative, or Bigger than 30? Here is how to handle it so you never get stuck:

🏦 The "Piggy Bank Borrow" (Shamsi → Miladi)

What if you do Day − 9 or Day − 10 and get 0 or a negative number (like 6 − 10 = −4)?

  1. There is no "Day 0" or "Day −4" on a calendar!
  2. Step back 1 month from your Starter Month (e.g., Month 11 becomes Month 10; if it was Month 1, wrap back to Month 12).
  3. Open that month's piggy bank of 30 days and add 30 to your number: 30 + (−4) = 26!
  4. Example: 1405/08/06 → Starter Month 8+3=11. Day 6−10=−4 → Borrow 1 month (Month 10) and add 30 (Day 26) → 2026/10/26!

🪣 The "Overflow Cup" (Miladi → Shamsi)

What if you do Day + 9 or Day + 10 and get a number bigger than 30 (like 25 + 10 = 35)?

  1. Imagine each month is a cup that only holds 30 days!
  2. Pour 30 days to fill up the Starter Month and keep the leftover drops: 35 − 30 = 5.
  3. Pour those leftover 5 days into the next month (Starter Month + 1; if 12, wrap to 1)!
  4. Example: 2026/11/25 → Starter Month 11−3=8. Day 25+10=35 → Subtract 30 (Day 5) and move forward 1 month (Month 9) → 1405/09/05!

🎯 Going the Extra Mile: Optional 100% Accuracy-Proofing Formulas

OPTIONAL PRO MASTERY

The basic 3-step method gets you within ±1 day in two seconds. Why is it sometimes 1 day off? Because of three small calendar quirks: (1) Shamsi has six 31-day months in a row, (2) some borrowed months have 31 or 28 days instead of 30, and (3) leap years shift Nowruz between March 20 and March 21. Add these 3 optional mental tweaks at the end to hit 100% exact accuracy every time:

Formula A: The "Season Drift" Tweak (Fixing the 31-Day Month Streak)
In a standard year where Nowruz is March 21, the basic rule uses −9 (Months 1–6) and −10 (Months 7–12). To make the day subtractor 100% exact, apply this quick seasonal tweak to your final day:
🌱 Spring (Months 1, 2, 3): Exact subtractors are 11, 10, 10 Tweak Basic Day by: −2 (M1), −1 (M2), −1 (M3)
☀️ Summer (Months 4, 5, 6): Exact subtractors are 9, 9, 9 Tweak Basic Day by: 0 (Basic −9 is already exact!)
🍂 Autumn (Months 7, 8, 9): Exact subtractors are 8, 9, 9 Tweak Basic Day by: +2 (M7), +1 (M8), +1 (M9)
❄️ Winter (Months 10, 11, 12): Exact subtractors are 10, 11, 9* Tweak Basic Day by: 0 (M10), −1 (M11), +1 (M12*)

*Note on Month 12 (Esfand): Because February only has 28 days in a common year, Esfand's exact subtractor for March is −9 (a +1 tweak over basic −10). If the Miladi year is a leap year (Feb has 29 days), Esfand's subtractor stays −10 (0 tweak). For Miladi → Shamsi, reverse the signs!

Formula B: The "Knuckle Month" Borrow & Overflow Fix (31 or 28 vs. 30 Days)
In Step 3, the basic rule always adds or subtracts 30 when crossing a month boundary. If you borrowed or overflowed, check the actual size of the month you crossed through:
Shamsi → Miladi Borrow into a 31-Day Month
Happens when borrowing in Shamsi Months 1, 3, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11 (lands in Mar, May, Jul, Aug, Oct, Dec, Jan) Add +1 extra day (31 instead of 30)
Shamsi → Miladi Borrow into February
Happens in early Month 12 (Esfand 1–9, lands in Feb) Subtract 2 days (28 instead of 30; or −1 in leap year)
Miladi → Shamsi Overflow from Months 1–6
Shamsi Months 1–6 hold 31 days (occurs in Miladi Apr–Sep) Subtract 31 instead of 30 (−1 day on overflow)
Formula C: The "Nowruz Anchor" Leap-Year Shift (March 20 vs. March 21)
Every 4 years, Miladi's February 29 pulls Nowruz back to March 20, until a Shamsi leap year (30 Esfand) pushes Nowruz back to March 21. Knowing which day Nowruz falls on fixes the final 1-day leap shift:
1. Quick Leap-Year Anchor (1391–1420):
Memorize 1403 and count by 4s (with one 5-year jump after 1403):
1391, 1395, 1399, 1403, 1408, 1412, 1416, 1420 Shamsi Leap Years (Esfand = 30 days)
2. Universal Mod-33 Formula (Any Century):
Divide Shamsi Year by 33. It is a leap year if the remainder is: 1, 5, 9, 13, 17, 22, 26, or 30
3. The March 20 vs. March 21 Rule:
• 1 or 2 years after a Shamsi leap year (e.g. 1400, 1401 or 1404, 1405): Nowruz = March 21
• All other years (e.g. 1402, 1403, 1406, 1407, 1408): Nowruz = March 20 If March 20: −1 day (S→M) or +1 day (M→S)
